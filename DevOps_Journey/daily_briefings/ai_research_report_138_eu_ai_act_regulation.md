# AI Research Report #138 — EU AI Act & Global AI Regulation Landscape

## Overview
The European Union's AI Act, the world's first comprehensive AI regulation framework, entered into force in August 2024 with phased implementation through 2026–2027. This report examines the Act's provisions, enforcement timeline, and implications for developers and organizations deploying AI systems.

## The EU AI Act: Key Provisions

### Risk-Based Tiered System
| Risk Level | Examples | Obligations |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| **Unacceptable Risk** | Social scoring, manipulative subliminal techniques | Banned outright |
| **High Risk** | Medical devices, hiring tools, critical infrastructure | Conformity assessment, CE marking, registration |
| **Limited Risk** | Chatbots, emotion recognition systems | Transparency obligations — users must know they're interacting with AI |
| **Minimal Risk** | Spam filters, video games | No specific obligations |

### Timeline
- **August 2024**: Act entered into force
- **February 2025**: Bans on unacceptable-risk AI take effect
- **August 2026**: High-risk AI obligations begin; member states must establish regulatory sandboxes
- **2027**: Full implementation including general-purpose AI (GPAI) provisions

### GPAI Model Requirements
Models deemed to have "systemic impact" face:
- Copyright compliance documentation
- Detailed training data summaries
- Energy consumption reporting
- Risk assessment procedures

## The AI Omnibus Directive (November 2025)
A companion legislative package adopted on November 19, 2025, strengthening enforcement mechanisms and closing loopholes. Political agreement reached May 7, 2026; entered into force July 27, 2026.

## International Developments

### United States
- Executive Order 14110 (October 2024) — Safe, Secure, Trusted AI development
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework adopted across federal agencies
- State-level laws: Colorado AI Act, California AI privacy law

### China
- Generative AI regulations (August 2023) — Algorithm filing requirements
- Deep synthesis regulations (2023)
- Focused on content control and algorithm transparency

### Other Jurisdictions
- **UK**: Pro-innovation, sector-specific approach via DCMS white paper
- **Canada**: Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) under Bill C-27
- **Brazil**: LGPD expanded to cover AI with draft AI regulation in progress
- **Japan**: AI guidelines aligned with OECD principles

## Impact on Developers
1. **Documentation burden** — High-risk systems require technical documentation and conformity assessments
2. **Training data provenance** — Increasing demand for copyright-compliant training data
3. **Model cards & transparency** — Best practice becoming regulatory requirement
4. **Audit trails** — Logging AI decisions for compliance verification
5. **Human oversight** — Mandatory for high-risk applications

## Compliance Checklist for AI Products
- [ ] Classify risk level under AI Act
- [ ] Implement human oversight mechanisms for high-risk systems
- [ ] Document training data sources and preprocessing
- [ ] Establish model performance monitoring
- [ ] Create incident reporting procedures
- [ ] Conduct fundamental rights impact assessments
