## Overview

Neuromorphic computing — hardware designed to emulate biological neural circuits — has moved from academic curiosity to practical deployment in 2026. Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) process information as discrete "spikes" rather than continuous values, offering orders-of-magnitude improvements in energy efficiency for edge AI.

## How Neuromorphic Computing Differs

| Aspect | Traditional AI Hardware | Neuromorphic Hardware |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Computation** | Matrix multiplications | Event-driven spike processing |
| **Memory** | Separated (Von Neumann) | Memory-compute integration |
| **Power** | Watts to kilowatts | Milliwatts to microwatts |
| **Latency** | Fixed clock cycles | Asynchronous, event-based |
| **Learning** | Backpropagation | Spike-timing-dependent plasticity |

- First AI chip with on-chip learning
- Event-based vision processing
- Sub-milliwatt power consumption

## Spiking Neural Network Algorithms

### Learning Rules
- **STDP (Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity)** — Biologically plausible learning
- **Hebbian learning** — "Neurons that fire together wire together"
- **Reward-modulated STDP** — Combines unsupervised and supervised learning

### Encoding Schemes
- **Rate coding** — Frequency of spikes represents signal
- **Temporal coding** — Exact spike timing encodes information
- **Surrogate gradients** — Enables backpropagation through spiking neurons

### Training Approaches
1. **Direct encoding** — Train SNN directly, no conversion needed
2. **Conversion from ANNs** — Convert trained artificial networks to spiking
3. **Surrogate gradient descent** — Differentiable approximation of spikes
